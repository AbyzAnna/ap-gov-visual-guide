Topic 3.13

Affirmative Action

Essential QuestionHow has affirmative action shaped the Supreme Court's restriction or protection of the civil rights of minorities?
In one minute

Affirmative action refers to policies that give preference or extra consideration to groups that faced discrimination, to increase diversity in jobs and education. Presidents Kennedy and Johnson created the policies. In Regents of the University of California v. Bakke (1978), the Court banned racial quotas but allowed race as one factor. Later cases narrowed it — and in 2023 the Court ended race-conscious college admissions.

1Seeking Diversity

Supporters argue

  • Remedies past and ongoing discrimination
  • Diversity benefits all students and workplaces
  • Expands opportunity
  • Creates role models & leaders from all groups
VS

Opponents argue

  • Any racial classification violates equal protection (14th Am.) & the Civil Rights Act
  • 'Reverse discrimination' against qualified applicants
  • Stigmatizes beneficiaries
  • Use class-based (income) preferences instead

Presidents Kennedy (Executive Order 10925, 1961) and Johnson (EO 11246, 1965) required federal contractors to take 'affirmative action' to ensure equal opportunity.

2The Supreme Court and Affirmative Action

Supporting case

Regents of the University of California v. Bakke (1978)

5–4
Fourteenth Amendment equal protection · Civil Rights Act Title VI
Facts
Allan Bakke, a white applicant, was rejected twice by UC Davis Medical School, which reserved 16 of 100 seats for minority students. His scores were higher than some admitted under the special program.
Constitutional question
Does a racial quota in admissions violate equal protection?
Decision
Quotas are unconstitutional — Bakke was admitted — BUT Justice Powell said race may be considered as one factor to achieve diversity.
Why it matters
Set the framework for decades: no quotas, but holistic consideration of race allowed.
Memory hook: Bakke = Ban quotas, but race can be a factor.
1978
Bakke — no quotas; race may be a factor
2003
Grutter v. Bollinger — Michigan Law School's holistic race consideration upheld; Gratz v. Bollinger — undergraduate points system struck down
2014
Schuette v. BAMN — states may ban affirmative action by ballot (e.g., Michigan, California's Prop 209)
2016
Fisher v. University of Texas — UT's plan upheld under strict scrutiny
2023
Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard / UNC — race-conscious admissions violate equal protection
Update since the book (2021)

The book (2021) predates SFFA v. Harvard (2023), which effectively ended race-based affirmative action in college admissions. Applicants may still discuss how race affected their lives in essays.

AP Exam Tip

Affirmative action cases are judged under strict scrutiny because they use racial classifications.
