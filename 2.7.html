Topic 2.7

Presidential Communication

Essential QuestionHow has communication technology changed the president's relationship with the American people and other branches?
In one minute

Presidents use the bully pulpit (T. Roosevelt's phrase) to 'go public' — appealing directly to the people to pressure Congress. The State of the Union Address is a key moment. Technology — from radio 'fireside chats' to TV to social media — has made communication more direct and constant.

2State of the Union

Article II requires the president to 'from time to time give to the Congress Information of the State of the Union.' Washington and Adams delivered it in person; Jefferson sent it in writing; Wilson revived the in-person speech. Today it's a televised prime-time address used to set the policy agenda and rally public support.

AP Exam Tip

Going public can backfire: it can harden opposition, and presidents' speeches don't always move opinion. Also note the 24-hour news cycle and fragmented media mean presidents reach mostly their own supporters.
