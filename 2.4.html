Topic 2.4

Roles and Powers of the President

Essential QuestionHow can a president implement a policy agenda?
In one minute

Presidents use formal powers (written in Article II: veto, commander in chief, treaties, appointments) and informal powers (executive orders, executive agreements, signing statements, bargaining and persuasion) to achieve their policy agenda. The framers wanted an energetic but limited executive.

1The Framers' Vision

After the weak Articles (no executive) and a tyrannical king, the framers compromised: a single executive elected indirectly for four-year terms, with real but checked powers. Article II's opening line — 'The executive Power shall be vested in a President' — is vague, which later presidents used to expand power.

  • Qualifications: 35 years old, natural-born citizen, 14 years' residency.
  • Chosen by the Electoral College.
  • Term limit: two terms (22nd Amendment, 1951).

2Formal vs Informal Powers

FORMAL (in the Constitution)

  • Veto and pocket veto of bills
  • Commander in Chief of the armed forces
  • Negotiate treaties (Senate ratifies, 2/3)
  • Appoint judges, ambassadors, cabinet (Senate confirms)
  • Grant pardons & reprieves
  • Deliver State of the Union; call Congress into special session
  • Receive ambassadors (recognize nations)
VS

INFORMAL (developed through practice)

  • Executive orders — directives with force of law to the bureaucracy (e.g., Truman desegregating the military)
  • Executive agreements — deals with foreign leaders without Senate approval
  • Signing statements — notes on how the president will interpret/enforce a law
  • Bargaining and persuasion — lobbying Congress, phone calls, 'going public'
  • Executive privilege — keeping some communications confidential
  • Setting the policy agenda
From the book

The line-item veto (letting the president veto parts of spending bills) was passed by Congress in 1996 but struck down in Clinton v. City of New York (1998) — it violated the presentment clause.

3Presidential Roles

Chief executive

Runs the bureaucracy; enforces laws.

Commander in chief

Directs the military.

Chief diplomat

Treaties, executive agreements, recognition.

Chief legislator

Proposes laws; vetoes.

Head of state

Ceremonial leader of the nation.

Party leader

Shapes the party's message and campaigns.

AP Exam Tip

Know examples: an executive order (DACA, 2012), an executive agreement (Paris Agreement), a veto threat used as leverage, and a signing statement.
