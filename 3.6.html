Topic 3.6

Amendments: Balancing Individual Freedom with Public Order and Safety

Essential QuestionHow has the Supreme Court attempted to balance claims of individual freedom with laws and enforcement procedures that promote public order and safety?
In one minute

The Court balances liberty and safety in the Eighth Amendment (no cruel and unusual punishment, no excessive bail), the Second Amendment (gun regulation), and the Fourth Amendment (no unreasonable searches and seizures). Colonists' anger at British writs of assistance inspired the Fourth Amendment; today's fights involve phones and metadata.

1Cruel and Unusual Punishment & Excessive Bail (8th Amendment)

1972
Furman v. Georgia — death penalty as applied was arbitrary; executions paused
1976
Gregg v. Georgia — death penalty reinstated with guided procedures
2002
Atkins v. Virginia — no executing people with intellectual disabilities
2005
Roper v. Simmons — no executing those under 18 when they committed the crime
2010/2012
Graham v. Florida, Miller v. Alabama — limits on life without parole for juveniles

The Court reads 'cruel and unusual' through 'evolving standards of decency.' Bail can't be set higher than needed to ensure a defendant returns for trial.

2Individual Rights and the Second Amendment

Even after Heller, the Court accepts regulations like bans on guns for felons and the mentally ill, and in sensitive places like schools. Balancing public safety (mass shootings) and individual rights remains one of the most polarized issues.
