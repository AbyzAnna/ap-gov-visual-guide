Topic 3.5

Second Amendment: Right to Bear Arms

Essential QuestionTo what extent does the Supreme Court's interpretation of the Second Amendment reflect a commitment to individual liberty?
In one minute

The Second Amendment (1791) says: 'A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed.' For decades the debate was whether it protects an individual right or a collective (militia) right. District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) said it protects an individual right to keep a handgun at home for self-defense. Congress has passed gun laws since 1934.

1Founding Principles and Bearing Arms

Individual-right view

  • The 'right of the people' means individuals
  • Self-defense is a natural right
  • Gun-rights groups (NRA)
  • Adopted in Heller (2008)
VS

Collective-right (militia) view

  • The opening clause links the right to a state militia
  • Allows broad regulation
  • Gun-control groups
  • Reflected in U.S. v. Miller (1939)

The founders distrusted standing armies and relied on citizen militias; English common law recognized a right to arms for self-defense.

2The Second Amendment and Gun Policy

1934
National Firearms Act — taxes/registration for machine guns and sawed-off shotguns (response to gangster violence)
1939
U.S. v. Miller — upheld NFA; sawed-off shotgun not related to a militia
1968
Gun Control Act — after the assassinations of MLK and RFK; licensing dealers; bans sales to felons
1993
Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act — background checks; named for James Brady, shot in the 1981 Reagan assassination attempt
1994–2004
Federal assault-weapons ban (expired)
2008
District of Columbia v. Heller — individual right; DC handgun ban struck down
2010
McDonald v. Chicago — incorporated the 2nd Amendment to states (Topic 3.7)
Supporting case

District of Columbia v. Heller (2008)

5–4
Second Amendment
Facts
Washington, D.C. banned handguns and required other guns in homes to be disassembled or trigger-locked. Special police officer Dick Heller challenged it.
Constitutional question
Does the Second Amendment protect an individual right to possess firearms, unconnected to militia service?
Decision
Yes. Justice Scalia: individuals have a right to keep a handgun in the home for self-defense. But the right is not unlimited — bans on felons possessing guns or on guns in schools remain valid.
Why it matters
First ruling clearly recognizing an individual right. Since D.C. is federal, it didn't yet apply to states — that came in McDonald.
Memory hook: Heller = Handgun at Home is protected (federal enclave).
Update since the book (2021)

NYSRPA v. Bruen (2022) struck down New York's concealed-carry permit rule and required gun laws to fit the nation's 'historical tradition'; U.S. v. Rahimi (2024) upheld disarming people under domestic-violence restraining orders.
