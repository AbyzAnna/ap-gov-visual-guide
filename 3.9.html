Topic 3.9

Amendments: Due Process and the Right to Privacy

Essential QuestionTo what extent are states limited by the due process clause from infringing upon individuals' rights to privacy?
In one minute

The Constitution never uses the word 'privacy,' but the Court found a right to privacy in the 'penumbras' of the Bill of Rights in Griswold v. Connecticut (1965). Using substantive due process (some rights are so fundamental government can't infringe them), Roe v. Wade (1973) extended privacy to a woman's choice to have an abortion. Congress's Hyde Amendment (1976) bans federal Medicaid funding for most abortions.

1Substantive Due Process

The due process clauses protect not just procedures but certain fundamental liberties from government interference, even if not listed. Critics argue unelected judges shouldn't create unlisted rights.

3Required Case: Roe v. Wade

REQUIRED CASE

Roe v. Wade (1973)

7–2
Fourteenth Amendment due process clause — right to privacy
Facts
'Jane Roe' (Norma McCorvey), a pregnant single woman in Texas, challenged a state law banning abortion except to save the mother's life.
Constitutional question
Does the Constitution's right to privacy protect a woman's decision to have an abortion?
Decision
Yes. Justice Harry Blackmun: the right to privacy (from Griswold, via 14th Am. due process) includes the abortion decision, balanced against state interests that grow during pregnancy — the trimester framework: 1st trimester, no state ban; 2nd, regulation for the mother's health; 3rd (viability), states may ban except to protect the mother's life or health.
Why it matters
Extended privacy/substantive due process; became one of the most divisive rulings in U.S. history, mobilizing social movements on both sides.
Memory hook: Roe = Right to privacy Reaches abortion (trimesters).
Update since the book (2021)

Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022) overruled Roe and Casey, holding the Constitution does not confer a right to abortion and returning the issue to the states and their voters. The College Board still lists Roe as a required case — know BOTH: what Roe held and that Dobbs overturned it.
