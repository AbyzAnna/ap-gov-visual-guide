Topic 2.13

Discretionary and Rule-Making Authority

Essential QuestionHow does the federal bureaucracy use delegated discretionary authority to make and implement rules?
In one minute

Congress writes broad laws and gives agencies delegated discretionary authority to fill in the details. Agencies make rules through a formal process under the Administrative Procedures Act (1946): proposed rules appear in the Federal Register, the public gets a notice-and-comment opportunity, and final rules are collected in the Code of Federal Regulations. Independent regulatory agencies like the SEC and FEC regulate whole sectors.

1Delegated Discretionary Authority

Congress passes broad law
→
Agency gets discretion
→
Detailed rules with force of law

Congress lacks the time and expertise to write every detail, so it delegates. Agencies decide how to carry out laws — e.g., the EPA decides what level of a pollutant is 'safe' under the Clean Air Act.

Update since the book (2021)

In Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (2024), the Court overruled Chevron deference — courts no longer automatically defer to an agency's reasonable interpretation of an ambiguous law. This narrows agency discretion.
