Topic 2.9

Legitimacy of the Judicial Branch

Essential QuestionHow has the Supreme Court's use of judicial review in conjunction with life tenure led to debates about the legitimacy of the court?
In one minute

The Court relies on precedent and stare decisis ('let the decision stand') for stability, but it sometimes overturns past rulings. Because justices are unelected with life tenure, people debate whether the Court is legitimate when it makes major policy. Its power depends on public trust, since it can't enforce its own rulings.

1Common Law and Precedent

Precedent

A prior court ruling used as a guide for later cases.

Stare decisis

'Let the decision stand' — courts follow precedent for predictability.

Binding precedent

Must be followed by lower courts in the same jurisdiction (Supreme Court rulings bind all).

Persuasive precedent

Rulings from other jurisdictions courts may consider but needn't follow.

The U.S. inherited common law from England — law built from judges' decisions over time.

Update since the book (2021)

Dobbs (2022) and Loper Bright v. Raimondo (2024, ending Chevron deference) show the Roberts Court's willingness to overturn long-standing precedents — fueling today's legitimacy debate.

3Debates About Legitimacy

Arguments the Court is legitimate

  • Protects minority rights from majorities
  • Independence (life tenure) prevents political pressure
  • Rooted in the Constitution & Fed 78
  • Follows precedent most of the time
VS

Arguments questioning legitimacy

  • Unelected justices make policy
  • Life tenure + longer lifespans = decades of influence
  • Appointments are increasingly partisan
  • Overturning precedents looks political

Chief Justice John Roberts (since 2005) has said judges are like 'umpires' calling balls and strikes. Public approval of the Court has fallen to historic lows since 2021, per Gallup.

“John Marshall has made his decision, now let him enforce it.”

— Attributed to Andrew Jackson on Worcester v. Georgia, 1832 — the Court can't enforce its own rulings
