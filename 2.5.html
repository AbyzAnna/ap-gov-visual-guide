Topic 2.5

Checks on the Presidency

Essential QuestionHow could the president's agenda contribute to confrontations with Congress?
In one minute

The president leads a team — the Cabinet, the Executive Office of the President, the chief of staff — but Congress and the courts check presidential power. The Senate confirms appointments (including ambassadors and the Joint Chiefs of Staff), Congress controls money and can override vetoes and impeach, and the courts review executive actions. Clashes are common, especially under divided government.

3Confrontations with Congress

  • Divided government → more vetoes, slower confirmations, more executive orders.
  • Presidents use the veto threat as bargaining leverage.
  • Lame-duck periods weaken presidents' influence.
  • The 22nd Amendment's term limit means second-term presidents face shrinking leverage.
