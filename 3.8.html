Topic 3.8

Amendments: Due Process and the Rights of the Accused

Essential QuestionTo what extent are states limited by the due process clause from infringing upon individual rights?
In one minute

Procedural due process means government must follow fair, established procedures before taking life, liberty, or property. The 4th, 5th, 6th, and 8th Amendments protect the accused: the exclusionary rule (Mapp v. Ohio), Miranda warnings (Miranda v. Arizona, with a public safety exception), and the right to an attorney (Gideon v. Wainwright). Schools need only reasonable suspicion (New Jersey v. T.L.O.).

1Procedural Due Process

Fair procedures: notice of charges, a hearing, an impartial judge, the right to present evidence. It also applies outside criminal courts — e.g., students facing suspension get notice and a hearing (Goss v. Lopez, 1975).

Remember

Procedural due process = how government acts (fair process). Substantive due process = whether government can act at all (fundamental rights like privacy, Topic 3.9).

4th

Mapp v. Ohio (1961)

Police searched Dollree Mapp's home without a valid warrant. Exclusionary rule — illegally obtained evidence can't be used — applied to the states.

5th

Miranda v. Arizona (1966)

Ernesto Miranda confessed without being told his rights. Police must warn suspects of the right to remain silent and to an attorney before custodial interrogation.

5th

Public safety exception

New York v. Quarles (1984): police may ask questions before Miranda warnings when public safety is at risk (e.g., where's the gun?).

4th

New Jersey v. T.L.O. (1985)

Schools may search students with reasonable suspicion rather than probable cause.

3Required Case: Gideon v. Wainwright

REQUIRED CASE

Gideon v. Wainwright (1963)

9–0
Sixth Amendment right to counsel · Fourteenth Amendment due process
Facts
Clarence Earl Gideon was charged with breaking into a Florida pool hall — a felony. Too poor to hire a lawyer, he asked for one; Florida only appointed counsel in capital cases. He defended himself, was convicted, and wrote a handwritten petition from prison to the Supreme Court.
Constitutional question
Does the Sixth Amendment's right to counsel apply to state felony defendants who can't afford a lawyer?
Decision
Yes. Justice Black: the right to counsel is fundamental to a fair trial and is incorporated through the 14th Amendment. States must provide lawyers to poor defendants in felony cases. (Overturned Betts v. Brady, 1942.) Gideon was retried with a lawyer and acquitted.
Why it matters
Created the public defender system; a landmark of selective incorporation and procedural due process.
Memory hook: Gideon = Gets a lawyer (6th Amendment), even if poor.

4Contemporary Procedural Due Process

  • Post-9/11: detainees at Guantánamo Bay — Hamdi v. Rumsfeld (2004) and Boumediene v. Bush (2008) required some due process/habeas corpus.
  • The USA FREEDOM Act (2015) ended NSA bulk collection of phone metadata, requiring requests for specific records.
  • Debates continue about plea bargains, cash bail, and public-defender caseloads.
