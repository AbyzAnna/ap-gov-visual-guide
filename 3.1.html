Topic 3.1

The Bill of Rights

Essential QuestionHow does the U.S. Constitution protect individual liberties and rights, and what rights are protected in the Bill of Rights?
In one minute

The Bill of Rights (1791) — the first ten amendments — protects civil liberties: freedoms from government interference. Originally it limited only the national government. Courts constantly balance individual liberty against the public interest (safety, order, health).

1Civil Liberties vs Civil Rights

Civil LIBERTIES

  • Freedoms FROM government
  • Limits on what government can do to you
  • Mostly in the Bill of Rights
  • Example: free speech, free exercise of religion, protection from unreasonable searches
VS

Civil RIGHTS

  • Protection from DISCRIMINATION
  • Government must treat groups equally
  • 14th Amendment equal protection + civil rights laws
  • Example: right not to be segregated or denied a job because of race or sex

AP Exam Tip

Memory trick: 1 speak, 2 bear arms, 3 no troops at home, 4 search, 5 silence & due process, 6 speedy trial & lawyer, 7 civil jury, 8 no cruel punishment, 9 more rights, 10 states' powers.

3A Culture of Civil Liberties

Rights are not absolute. Courts weigh individual liberty against the public interest. Example from the book: during the COVID-19 pandemic, many wore masks for the public good, while others felt mandates infringed on individual rights. Other examples: speech can be limited when it threatens imminent danger; searches are allowed with probable cause.

Remember

The Bill of Rights originally applied only to the federal government (Barron v. Baltimore, 1833). The 14th Amendment's due process clause later applied most of it to the states — see Topic 3.7, selective incorporation.
