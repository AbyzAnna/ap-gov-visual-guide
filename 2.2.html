Topic 2.2

Structures, Powers, and Functions of Congress

Essential QuestionHow do the structures, powers, and functions of Congress affect the policymaking process?
In one minute

Most of Congress's work happens in committees led by party leaders. The House is run by the Speaker and the powerful Rules Committee; the Senate relies on unanimous consent and allows the filibuster, which can only be stopped by cloture (60 votes). A bill must survive committees, floor votes in both chambers, a conference committee, and the president. Congress also writes the budget — split into mandatory and discretionary spending.

2Committees — The Workhorses

TypeWhat it doesExample
Standing committeesPermanent; handle bills in a policy area; hold hearingsHouse Ways and Means (taxes); Senate Judiciary; Appropriations
Select committeesTemporary; investigate a specific issueJan. 6 Committee; Watergate Committee
Joint committeesMembers of both houses; studies/oversightJoint Economic Committee
Conference committeesTemporary; reconcile House & Senate versions of a billUsed for major budget bills

Committee membership is proportional to party strength; chairs come from the majority party. Most bills die in committee ('pigeonholed').

3Rules Unique to Each Chamber

House rules

  • Rules Committee: decides when a bill reaches the floor and sets debate limits & whether amendments are allowed ('traffic cop')
  • Committee of the Whole: the full House acts as a committee with a lower quorum (100) to speed debate
  • Discharge petition: 218 signatures force a bill out of a committee
  • Ways and Means Committee: writes tax bills
VS

Senate rules

  • Filibuster: unlimited debate to delay or kill a bill
  • Cloture rule: 3/5 (60 senators) to end debate
  • Unanimous consent: agreements to move business; one senator can object
  • Hold: a senator's informal block on a bill or nominee
  • Amendments need not be germane — so riders get attached

5Generating a Budget

Feb.
President submits budget proposal (prepared by OMB)
Spring
Budget committees set a budget resolution; CBO provides estimates
Summer
12 appropriations bills move through committees
Oct. 1
Fiscal year begins — if no bills pass: continuing resolution or government shutdown

A deficit is when spending exceeds revenue in one year; the accumulated deficits are the national debt. Because mandatory spending grows automatically, Congress controls only about a third of spending each year.
