Topic 2.11

Checks on the Judicial Branch

Essential QuestionWhat issues lead to debates about the legitimacy of the Court, and how can other branches limit Supreme Court power?
In one minute

Debates over judicial activism vs judicial restraint reflect worries about unelected judges making policy. The other branches check the courts through appointments and confirmations, impeachment, changing jurisdiction or the number of justices, passing new laws or amendments, and by how they enforce (or don't) rulings.

1Judicial Activism vs Judicial Restraint

Judicial activism

  • Courts should actively correct injustices and check other branches
  • Willing to overturn laws and precedents
  • Examples: Brown v. Board, Roe v. Wade, Citizens United, Dobbs (depends who you ask)
VS

Judicial restraint

  • Courts should defer to elected branches
  • Overturn laws only if clearly unconstitutional
  • Respect precedent (stare decisis)
  • Plessy v. Ferguson is often cited as excessive deference
Common mistake

Activism ≠ liberal and restraint ≠ conservative. Activism is about striking down laws or precedents — conservative courts can be activist too.

Courts also limit themselves: they only hear real cases where plaintiffs have standing (they were directly harmed), and they avoid 'political questions.'
