Topic 3.11

Government Responses to Social Movements

Essential QuestionHow has the government responded to social movements?
In one minute

After the Civil War, the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments promised freedom, citizenship, and voting rights — but Jim Crow laws, poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses, and white primaries denied them, and Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) approved 'separate but equal.' The courts struck back with Brown v. Board of Education (1954), and Congress passed the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Voting Rights Act of 1965, the 24th Amendment, and Title IX.

1Reconstruction and Its Legacy

13th Amendment (1865)

Abolished slavery.

14th Amendment (1868)

Citizenship for all born here; due process; equal protection.

15th Amendment (1870)

Voting can't be denied based on race.

Civil Rights Act of 1875

Banned discrimination in public places — struck down in the Civil Rights Cases (1883): the 14th Amendment only limits STATE action, not private businesses.

Supporting case

Plessy v. Ferguson (1896)

7–1
Fourteenth Amendment — equal protection
Facts
Homer Plessy, who was 7/8 white, sat in a whites-only rail car in Louisiana to test the Separate Car Act and was arrested.
Constitutional question
Does state-mandated racial segregation violate equal protection?
Decision
No — 'separate but equal' facilities are constitutional. Justice John Marshall Harlan dissented alone: 'Our Constitution is color-blind.'
Why it matters
Legalized Jim Crow segregation for nearly 60 years until Brown.
Memory hook: Plessy = Permitted segregation.

2The Courts Assert Equality — Required Case: Brown v. Board

REQUIRED CASE

Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka, Kansas (1954)

9–0
Fourteenth Amendment — equal protection clause
Facts
Linda Brown, a Black third-grader in Topeka, had to travel far to a segregated Black school instead of the nearby white school. The NAACP, led by Thurgood Marshall, combined her case with others from four states. They used social-science evidence (the Clarks' 'doll test') showing segregation harmed Black children.
Constitutional question
Does racial segregation in public schools violate the equal protection clause?
Decision
Yes. Chief Justice Earl Warren, for a unanimous Court: 'Separate educational facilities are inherently unequal.' Overturned Plessy for public education. Brown II (1955) ordered desegregation 'with all deliberate speed.'
Why it matters
Ended legal segregation in schools; sparked the modern civil rights movement; a model of the Court protecting minority rights against majorities.
Memory hook: Brown = Breaks 'separate but equal.' Unanimous, 9–0.
AP Exam Tip

Brown + Letter from a Birmingham Jail + Civil Rights Act of 1964 is a powerful evidence trio for any civil rights essay.

3Legislating Toward Equality

Law / AmendmentWhat it did
Civil Rights Act of 1964Title II bans discrimination in public accommodations (hotels, restaurants) — upheld under the commerce clause (Heart of Atlanta Motel). Title VI: no discrimination in federally funded programs. Title VII: no job discrimination by race, color, religion, sex, national origin (created the EEOC).
24th Amendment (1964)Banned poll taxes in federal elections.
Voting Rights Act of 1965Banned literacy tests; federal examiners; preclearance for states with histories of discrimination. Black registration in Mississippi rose from ~7% to ~60%.
Fair Housing Act (1968)Banned housing discrimination.
Title IX (Education Amendments of 1972)No sex discrimination in federally funded education.
Americans with Disabilities Act (1990)Banned disability discrimination; required accessibility.
Update since the book (2021)

Shelby County v. Holder (2013) struck down the VRA's preclearance formula (Section 4), letting states change voting rules without federal approval.

White flight — white families moving to suburbs or private schools — undercut school integration in many cities (see 3.12).
