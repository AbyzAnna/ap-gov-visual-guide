Topic 1.4

Challenges of the Articles of Confederation

Essential QuestionHow did the provisions of the Articles of Confederation lead to debates over granting powers formerly reserved for states to the federal government?
In one minute

America's first constitution, the Articles of Confederation (drafted 1777, ratified 1781), created a confederation: a 'firm league of friendship' with a weak Congress and sovereign states. It couldn't tax, regulate commerce, or raise an army effectively. Shays' Rebellion (1786–87) exposed the danger and pushed leaders to call the Constitutional Convention.

1How the Articles Were Built

FOUNDATIONAL DOCUMENT

Articles of Confederation

Drafted 1777 · Ratified 1781 · Required foundational document
Big argument: After fighting a powerful king, Americans deliberately created a weak central government so states would remain sovereign.
  • Unicameral Congress; each state had one vote regardless of population.
  • No national executive and no national judiciary.
  • Major laws needed 9 of 13 states; amendments needed unanimous consent.
  • Congress could declare war, make treaties, borrow money, run a postal service — but could not enforce its decisions.

2An Ineffective Confederation — The Weaknesses

Weakness under the ArticlesResultConstitution's fix
No power to tax (could only request money)Couldn't pay war debts or soldiersCongress may lay and collect taxes (Art. I, Sec. 8)
No power to regulate interstate/foreign commerceStates taxed each other's goods; trade warsCommerce clause
No national army (relied on state militias)Couldn't put down rebellions or defend bordersCongress raises army & navy; president is commander in chief
No national currencyStates printed their own money; inflationCongress coins money
No executive or courtsNo one to enforce laws or settle state disputesArticle II & Article III
9/13 for laws; unanimous to amendGridlock; reform impossibleMajority vote; Article V amendment process

One success: the Northwest Ordinance (1787) set up a process for adding new states and banned slavery in the Northwest Territory.

AP Exam Tip

Shays' Rebellion is the go-to example of how the Articles' weakness (no army, no national power) led to the call for a stronger government.
