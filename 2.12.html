Topic 2.12

The Bureaucracy

Essential QuestionHow does the bureaucracy carry out the responsibilities of the federal government?
In one minute

The federal bureaucracy — about 2–3 million civilian employees in departments, agencies, commissions and government corporations — carries out laws. It writes regulations, issues fines, tests and approves products, and collects data. Relationships among agencies, congressional committees, and interest groups form iron triangles and issue networks. Hiring moved from the spoils system (patronage) to the merit system after the Pendleton Act (1883).

1Structure of the Bureaucracy

Cabinet departments (15)

Largest units, each headed by a Secretary (e.g., State, Defense, Treasury, Homeland Security, Education, Veterans Affairs, Transportation; Justice is led by the Attorney General).

Independent executive agencies

Outside departments but under the president: NASA, CIA, EPA.

Independent regulatory agencies/commissions

Make and enforce rules in a sector; led by bipartisan boards with fixed terms: SEC, FCC, FEC, Federal Reserve.

Government corporations

Businesses run by the government: U.S. Postal Service, Amtrak, FDIC.

3Iron Triangles & Issue Networks

An issue network is a looser web of people — agency officials, congressional staff, interest groups, think tanks, academics, journalists — who share expertise and interest in a policy area. Issue networks are more open and changeable than iron triangles.

4From Patronage to Merit

1829
Spoils system under Jackson — 'to the victor belong the spoils'; jobs given to party loyalists (patronage)
1881
President Garfield assassinated by a disappointed office-seeker
1883
Pendleton Civil Service Act — jobs based on exams & qualifications (merit system); created the Civil Service Commission
1939
Hatch Act limits political activity by federal employees
1978
Civil Service Reform Act — replaced the Commission with the Office of Personnel Management and the Merit Systems Protection Board
1993
National Performance Review (Clinton/Gore) — 'reinventing government' to cut waste
AP Exam Tip

Political appointees (top positions, ~4,000) still exist — chosen by the president. Most of the bureaucracy is career civil servants hired by merit.
