Topic 2.15

Policy and the Branches of Government

Essential QuestionTo what extent do the branches of government hold the bureaucracy accountable, given the competing interests of Congress, the president, and the federal courts?
In one minute

All three branches try to hold the bureaucracy accountable, but they often compete. Congress can use a legislative veto (struck down in INS v. Chadha, 1983) or the Congressional Review Act; the president uses appointments and orders; courts review whether agencies followed the Administrative Procedures Act. The Whistleblower Protection Act (1989) protects employees who report wrongdoing.

Legislative veto

A law letting one or both houses reject an agency action without passing a new law (and without presidential signature). Struck down in INS v. Chadha (1983) for violating bicameralism & presentment.

Whistleblower Protection Act (1989)

Protects federal employees who report fraud, waste, or abuse from retaliation.

Congressional Review Act (1996)

Congress can overturn a new rule by joint resolution (signed by the president).
